Privacy Policy

Ownio · last updated 28 October 2026

Ownio is a record of what you own, kept for the day you have to prove it. That record is unusually personal — it is a list of your possessions and what they are worth — so this page says exactly where every part of it lives and what, specifically, ever leaves your phone.

There is no account. Ownio never asks for your name, e-mail address, phone number or a password, and has no way to learn them. Nothing you document is tied to an identity, because there is no identity to tie it to.

What stays on your phone

All of it, by default. Your inventory lives in a database in the app's own storage and the photographs live as files beside it. Neither is uploaded, backed up to a server of ours, or readable by us.

  • Every room, item, value, quantity, condition, note and serial number.
  • Every photograph you take or import.
  • The property address and policy number, if you enter them for the report. These are used only to print them on the PDF you create, and are never transmitted anywhere.
  • Your currency, home type and the personal-property limit you type into the coverage check.

Ownio keeps no copy of its own anywhere else. Deleting the app deletes all of it from the phone. If iCloud Backup (or a computer backup) of your phone is on, that backup includes the app's data, as it does for any app — and because an inventory is worth most on the day the phone is gone with the house, Settings → Export inventory (from version 1.0.1) lets you keep a copy wherever you choose.

What leaves your phone, and when

Seven things, each for one purpose.

How the app is used, as counts: nine named events of our own, such as "a scan started" or "the paywall was shown", plus the events the Firebase library records by itself (first open, session start, screen views, app updates) with the device model, operating system and language. Your purchase receipt for a photograph pack, and the store's transaction identifier for it. Only when you buy additional photographs. So the purchase can be verified with Apple or Google, and so the same purchase cannot be credited twice. So a renewal's photographs arrive at once, a cancellation is shown correctly, and a refunded photograph pack can give back what was not yet spent. Each notice is checked against Apple's own records before anything changes.

Who processes a scanned photograph

Ownio's own server — which runs on Cloudflare — passes the photograph to an AI provider to be identified: Anthropic, or OpenAI if Anthropic does not answer for any reason. It returns the result to your phone. The photograph is not kept by our server. These providers process it under their own commercial API terms, which do not allow it to be used to train their models, and may keep it for a limited time for abuse monitoring (typically up to 30 days, longer only where their terms or the law require). Each of them, and Google for analytics, is bound by terms that protect the data at least as well as this policy does. If you would rather no photograph ever be sent, say "Not now" when Ownio asks, or add items by hand: the pencil button beside the camera opens the same form, and nothing about a hand-entered item leaves the phone.

What we keep on our server

Counters and a photograph balance, stored against your subscription's identifier (or, without a subscription, the anonymous installation identifier above). No photographs, no item names, no values, no inventory, and no request content of any kind is written to storage or to our logs.

  • Counters — how many scans have been made this day and this month, whether the subscription checked out, and — to stop abuse — how many requests an internet address has made in the last minute and day. These sit on a short expiry (a day or two at most).
  • Your subscription's status — its identifier, when the current period ends and whether it will renew, as Apple last reported them. Kept while you are a subscriber so every device on the subscription agrees.
  • Your photograph balance — how many photographs remain in the current subscription period, and how many you have bought and not yet spent. The purchased half is kept until you spend it and does not expire, because that is exactly what you paid for: photographs that outlive the week. It is a number, not a history — we do not keep a record of which scans it was spent on.
  • The transaction identifier of each photograph pack you buy, kept so the same purchase can never be credited twice. It is the store's reference for the sale, not a payment method; Ownio never sees your card, and Apple and Google do not give it to us.

What Ownio does not do

  • No advertising, and no advertising SDK.
  • No attribution SDK, and no advertising identifier. Ownio uses Firebase Analytics to count which screens are used, from a fixed list of nine events; it cannot record what you own, because there is no way in the app to attach that to an event.
  • No tracking across apps or websites, and no App Tracking Transparency prompt. The analytics library ships in the variant that has the advertising identifier removed, so there is nothing to ask for.
  • No location permission of any kind, and no access to your device's location. The country used for pricing comes from your language setting, not from where you are. Analytics requests, like any internet request, carry an IP address, from which Google can infer an approximate region (to about the city) for its reporting; that is inference by the recipient, not a location this app reads or sends.
  • No access to contacts, calendar, microphone, or your photo library beyond the individual images you choose to import.
  • No selling or sharing of personal information. There is nothing to sell.

Permissions the app asks for

  • Camera — to photograph the items you are documenting. Photographs are written to the app's own storage, not to your camera roll.
  • Photo library — only when you choose to import an image you already have. Ownio sees the images you pick and nothing else.

Children

Ownio is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect anything from them. It has no account, no profile and no social features.

Your rights

Because Ownio holds no account and stores your inventory only on your own device, you exercise every right yourself and immediately: Settings → Delete everything removes the entire inventory and all photographs from the device, along with any reports you exported to it. To have what our server holds for you cleared as well — the counters, your subscription's status and your photograph balance — write to the address below with the Order ID from Apple's purchase receipt e-mail, which is how we find it; most of it expires on its own, and we delete the rest on request. We keep only the records a purchase itself needs — that it was redeemed or refunded, and where its photographs moved — so that it cannot be redeemed twice and a refund still reaches it. They hold store transaction numbers and internal identifiers, nothing about your things, and are kept while the purchase could still be refunded or redeemed again. Erasing covers what is held at that moment: if you keep using Ownio, it starts again from there.

One warning, because it is your money rather than your data: any photographs you have bought and not yet spent are held against your subscription (or that same anonymous identifier), and erasing it erases them. Apple and Google do not restore a one-off purchase, so we could not give them back. If you ask us to clear your installation we will tell you what the balance is first, and only proceed once you confirm.

Changes

If this policy changes in a way that affects what leaves your phone, the updated date at the top will change and the new version will be published here before the change ships.
